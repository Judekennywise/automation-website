# School Systems Business — Strategy v2

This version fixes the five weaknesses in the original plan: crowded naming, hidden-AI risk, fake-agency voice, unfocused offering, and website-as-strategy thinking. The core insight from v1 survives: you sell better-run schools, not automation.

---

## 1. The Strategy in One Paragraph

You are a founder-led specialist who installs one high-ROI system — professional fee follow-up — into Lagos private schools, delivered through the channels schools already use (WhatsApp, SMS, email). You win your first three schools through pilots priced to say yes, sourced through proprietor networks and referrals, not through a website. Once a school trusts you with fees, you expand into admissions follow-up, report card assistance, and student monitoring. The website exists to make you look credible *after* a referral, not to generate leads.

---

## 2. Positioning

**Who you are:** A specialist who builds the operational systems behind well-run schools.

**Who you are not:** An AI automation agency, a software company with a "team," or a general consultant.

**One-line positioning:**

> I help private schools collect fees on time and communicate with parents professionally — without adding work for their staff.

Notice: singular "I," one specific outcome, one specific audience. You can widen this after you have clients. You cannot narrow it after you've promised transformation.

**Why fees first (the wedge):**

- Unpaid fees are an existential, quantifiable pain. A proprietor can calculate your value in naira within one conversation.
- The decision-maker (the proprietor/owner) feels this pain personally. Admissions and report cards are felt by staff; fees are felt by the person who signs the cheque.
- It's the shortest sales cycle and the lowest-risk system to run. A fee reminder that's slightly imperfect is embarrassing at worst. A student early-warning system that misses a struggling child is a reputational landmine.
- It creates a natural expansion path: once your system is trusted with money conversations, admissions and reports are easy upsells.

The other three demos (admissions assistant, report card assistant, early-warning system) become the **expansion menu**, introduced in term two of a client relationship — not the launch pitch.

---

## 3. The Honesty Framework (AI, Data, and Trust)

The original advice — never say "AI" — was half right. Here is the full rule:

**In marketing copy:** Lead with outcomes. "AI-powered" goes nowhere near the hero section. Correct.

**In sales conversations and product descriptions:** Be plainly honest about what generates drafts and messages. Specifically:

- Fee reminders and admissions replies: describe them as "automated messages your bursar reviews and approves." No one objects to this.
- Report card comments: the school must know, explicitly, that drafts are AI-generated and teachers review every one. Put this in writing. If a parent later learns comments about their child were machine-written and the school didn't know, that story travels through Lagos parent WhatsApp groups faster than any marketing you'll ever do. Sold honestly ("your teachers review and personalise every comment; the system just kills the blank page"), it's a strength.

**Data protection — your unfair advantage:**

You will handle student records, academic data, and parent contact details. Nigeria's Data Protection Act (NDPA 2023) applies. Most small vendors have no answer to "where does our data live?" You will. Prepare a one-page data handling summary covering:

- What data the system stores, and what it never stores
- Where it's hosted
- Who can access it
- That the school remains the data owner and can export or delete everything on request
- Your commitment that student data is never used to train models or shared with third parties

Bring this to every sales conversation *before* being asked. For conservative buyers choosing between you and a random freelancer, this single page closes deals.

## 4. Name

Skip EduFlow and its siblings — EduFlow alone collides with a YC-backed company acquired by Multiverse *and* an Indian school-management platform claiming 800+ institutions doing nearly your exact business. SchoolPilot, CampusFlow, and NexSchool have similar problems.

**Criteria for the name you pick:**

1. .com or .ng domain actually available (check before falling in love)
2. No existing edtech/school-software company using it (search "name + school + software")
3. Trademark-searchable in Nigeria
4. Pronounceable over a phone call in Lagos — proprietors refer by word of mouth
5. Doesn't box you into one product ("FeeReminder Ltd" dies when you expand)

**Two viable paths:**

**Path A — Your name (recommended for now):** "Jude [Surname] — School Systems." Founder-led services businesses close better under a real name. It's honest about your size, builds personal referral equity, and costs nothing. You can rebrand to a company name when you productise. This is what most successful consultants-turned-founders actually did.

**Path B — A coined name:** If you want a brand from day one, coin something rather than combining Edu/School/Class with Flow/Pilot/Bridge (all mined out). Directions to explore, *verifying availability yourself*: Yoruba or Nigerian-English roots (warm, local, defensible — e.g., something built on "kọ́" (to learn/teach) or "ilé-ìwé"), or short invented words. Spend a maximum of two hours on this. The name matters far less than the first pilot.

## 5. Go-to-Market (The Part the Original Skipped)

Lagos proprietors buy from people, not websites. Your actual acquisition plan:

**Step 1 — Land 2–3 pilot schools (this term).**
- Start with warm network: any proprietor, teacher, bursar, or parent you or your family knows. One introduction beats 1,000 site visitors.
- Pilot offer: "I'll set up the fee follow-up system for your school this term at [heavily reduced fee / cost only]. If it doesn't measurably improve your collections, you owe nothing further. In exchange: a testimonial and permission to reference your school."
- Deliver personally. Over-serve. These schools are your case studies, referral sources, and product feedback all at once.

**Step 2 — Work the proprietor networks.**
- NAPPS (National Association of Proprietors of Private Schools) chapters hold regular meetings. Attend, offer to present "How schools are losing fee revenue to poor follow-up — and what to do about it" (an education talk, not a pitch).
- Church, alumni, and community networks where proprietors cluster.
- Ask every pilot school proprietor: "Which two school owners do you know who struggle with fee collection?" Proprietors know each other.

**Step 3 — Make WhatsApp your product's home, not its enemy.**
The original doc listed WhatsApp as a problem. In Nigeria, WhatsApp *is* the parent channel. Your fee reminders, admissions replies, and updates should arrive where parents already are — via WhatsApp Business API where feasible, SMS as fallback. "Works through the WhatsApp your parents already use" is a selling line, not a concession.

**Step 4 — Term-cycle timing.**
Schools decide at term boundaries. Fee pain peaks in weeks 1–4 of each term (collection season) — that's your outreach window. Build and onboard during mid-term and holidays.

**Success metric for the next 90 days:** signed pilots, not site traffic. If you have zero pilots and a beautiful website, the plan failed. If you have three pilots and no website, the plan worked.

## 6. The Website (Credibility Asset, Not Lead Engine)

One clean page (plus a demo page) is enough. Its job: when a proprietor gets your name from a colleague and looks you up, they think "this person is real and specific."

### Hero

**Headline:**
# Fees Collected On Time. Parents Answered Promptly. Staff Freed to Focus on Students.

**Subheadline:**
I build the systems behind well-run private schools — starting with professional fee follow-up that improves collections without a single awkward phone call from your staff.

**CTA (concrete, not consultant-speak):**
**See the fee system in action — 15-minute demo** (link to Calendly/WhatsApp)

Background: a real Nigerian classroom or school office. No robots, no glowing circuit brains.

### Section 2 — The Problem (fee-focused)

> Every term, the pattern repeats. Reminders go out late or not at all. Your bursar spends days chasing payments over calls that strain relationships with the very parents you want to keep. Some fees simply slip through — and the school absorbs the loss.
>
> It isn't a staff problem. It's a systems problem.

### Section 3 — The System

**Professional Fee Follow-Up**
Timely, respectful payment reminders sent automatically through WhatsApp, SMS, or email — in your school's voice, on your school's schedule. Your bursary team sees everything, approves what goes out, and steps in only where a human touch is needed.

- Reminders parents don't resent
- A live view of who's paid, who's pending, who needs a call
- Nothing sent without your team's oversight

*How it works, honestly:* the system drafts and schedules the messages; your staff stay in control. Your data stays yours — here's [my data handling summary].

### Section 4 — Also Available for Established Clients

One short line each, clearly framed as expansion, not the pitch:

- **Admissions follow-up** — every enquiry answered promptly, no prospective parent forgotten
- **Report card assistance** — AI-drafted comments your teachers review and personalise, turning days of writing into hours of reviewing
- **Student support monitoring** — earlier visibility into students who may need help

(Note the report card line discloses the AI drafting openly. That's deliberate.)

### Section 5 — About (founder voice)

> Hi, I'm Jude.
>
> For the past three years I've built systems that quietly remove repetitive work from organisations. I now work exclusively with private schools, because that's where the gap between "buried in admin" and "running smoothly" changes real outcomes — for staff, for parents, and for students.
>
> I work with a small number of schools at a time and build every system personally. I'm currently taking on pilot schools for the [term] term.

Scarcity is honest here — and more persuasive than a fake "our team."

### Section 6 — Proof

No "testimonials coming soon" — that's a flashing sign reading *nobody has bought this*. Until you have quotes:

- Embed the fee system demo video directly
- State the pilot offer plainly: "I'm onboarding [2–3] pilot schools this term at reduced rates in exchange for honest feedback."

Replace with real testimonials and a mini case study ("Collections up X% in one term at a 400-student school in [area]") the moment pilot one delivers.

## 7. Brand Voice (unchanged from v1 — it was right)

Calm. Professional. Education-first. Human. Outcome-focused.

Avoid in marketing: "AI-powered," "cutting-edge," "workflow automation," "intelligent agents."
Use instead: on-time collections, less administration, faster parent communication, more time for teaching, systems your staff control.

One addition: **never hide the AI in a sales conversation.** The marketing leads with outcomes; the sales conversation is transparent about the machinery. That combination — outcome-led *and* honest — is rarer than either alone, and it's what makes you referable.

---

## 8. The 90-Day Plan

**Weeks 1–2:** Finalise the fee system into a polished, deliverable pilot. Write the one-page data handling summary. Choose the name (2 hours max) and put up the one-page site.

**Weeks 3–6:** Warm outreach. List every school connection you have. Aim: 10 demo conversations, 2–3 signed pilots. Attend one NAPPS or proprietor gathering.

**Weeks 7–12:** Deliver pilots exceptionally. Track collection numbers from day one — that data is your future case study. Ask each pilot proprietor for two introductions.

**End of term:** Convert pilots to paying clients, publish the first case study, raise prices, and open the expansion conversation (admissions or report cards) with your happiest school.

## What Changed from v1, and Why

| v1 | v2 | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "EduFlow" and similar names | Founder name or verified coined name | EduFlow has multiple existing companies, including a direct competitor |
| Never mention AI | Outcome-led marketing, honest sales conversations | Hidden AI in report cards is a trust time-bomb; disclosed, it's a strength |
| "We" / transformation company | Founder-led "I", pilot scarcity | Credibility gap for a solo founder; honesty sells better to conservative buyers |
| Four equal offerings | Fee follow-up wedge + expansion menu | Unknown vendors selling everything sell nothing; fees = clearest ROI |
| WhatsApp as a problem | WhatsApp as the delivery channel | It's where Nigerian parents already are |
| "Testimonials coming soon" | Demo + explicit pilot offer | Placeholders advertise the absence of customers |
| Website as the strategy | Referrals/NAPPS/pilots as the strategy; website as credibility | Lagos proprietors buy from people they've met |
| No data story | NDPA-aware data handling one-pager | Student data compliance is a deal-maker with serious schools |
